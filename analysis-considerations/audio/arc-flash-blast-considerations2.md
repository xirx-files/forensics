In an electrical failure involving a Public Address (PA) system, an arc flash and an electrocution (electric shock) are two distinct but often simultaneous hazards. If a high-voltage fault occurs in the amplifier or power supply, the energy can travel through the wired microphone to the person holding it and manifest as both a physical blast and a through-body shock. [1] 
## Arc Flash Blast vs. Electrocution

* Electrocution (Electric Shock): This is the flow of current through the body. In a PA system, if the microphone's chassis becomes energized due to a fault, the person holding it becomes part of the electrical circuit. This can cause muscle contractions (preventing them from letting go), cardiac arrest, or internal tissue damage.
* Arc Flash: This is a "short circuit through the air" that creates an intense explosion of light and heat (up to 19,000°C–20,000°C).
* Arc Blast: This is the pressure wave following the flash. When metal (like copper in the mic cable) vaporizes, it expands by up to 67,000 times its original volume, creating a supersonic shockwave. [2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7] 

## Observations at 1.3 Metres
A distance of 1.3m is within the "restricted approach boundary" for many high-energy systems. In such an incident, you would expect: [8] 

* Visual: A blinding flash of light, similar to a welding arc, which can cause permanent retinal damage or "arc eye" (a sensation like sand in the eyes).
* Auditory: A deafening bang or "concussive blast" exceeding 140–160 decibels, capable of rupturing eardrums.
* Physical: A pressure wave that can knock a person off their feet or collapse lungs.
* Thermal: Severe burns from radiant heat or "plasma" (superheated gas) and molten metal shrapnel expelled at speeds over 1,100 km/h.
* Olfactory/Sensory: A distinct smell of ozone and vaporized metal, often accompanied by a burning sensation in the sinuses. [5, 9, 10, 11, 12, 13] 

## Projectile Risk: The "Pendant" Scenario
Yes, an electrical charge or arc blast can propel a pendant like a projectile.

* Electromagnetic Propulsion: Large fault currents create massive magnetic fields. If a metallic pendant is within this field, "Lorentz forces" can physically jerk or propel the jewelry with enough force to snap chains or cause it to strike the wearer.
* Pressure Wave (Arc Blast): The explosive expansion of air and vaporized metal acts like a physical punch. This pressure can easily dislodge and propel loose objects, including jewelry or equipment components, at high velocities.
* Thermal Fusion: If the pendant is part of the electrical path (e.g., hanging near the microphone), the heat can instantly melt the metal, causing it to "spray" or embed into the skin. [5, 9, 12, 14, 15] 

For these reasons, safety standards like NFPA 70E and safety experts at [Tyndale USA](https://tyndaleusa.com/blog/2024/05/17/understanding-what-accessories-should-not-cross-an-arc-flash-boundary/) strictly prohibit wearing conductive jewelry (necklaces, rings, watches) when working near energized equipment.
