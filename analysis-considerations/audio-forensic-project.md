# Audio Forensic Project

This project's goal is to develop a comprehensive python script to dump verbose output of audio analysis. automatation is prioritised over human interpretation to remove as many aspects of bias and human error. 

## Background

There was a highly suspicious assassination event at an outdoor amphitheatre attended by 3000 individuals. The only real evidence is the video and its embedded audio that was recorded independently on 17 different phone camera devices. some video recordings are highly pixelated; two videos do not have the targetted subject in frame. several samples have high ambience noise.


## Video impact analysis

a python script has been developed to analyse kenetic energy between frames to illustrate vectors of movement. only 1 video was recorded at 60 fps and all others were at 30 fps. the purpose of this is kenetic energy analysis is determine the frame of impact as accurately as possible which can later be incorporated into TDOA.

note, only one video file's embedded audio is at 48K sample rate, whilst all others are at 44100.


## Camera location analysis

a still photo frame has been exported from each video file and run through perspective-n-point process to determine the location of phone camera. this is supported by a world-coordinate-system which maps to google earths lat/lon placemarkers.


## PA System

the PA system with included the following wired components:
1. 1 wired microphone held by the keynote speaker
2. 1 wired microphone on a microphone stand for an audience member to ask questions
3. 4 wired speakers directed at different locations in the amphitheatre


## Exporting the key segment

each video varies in duration, some just a few seconds and others long. however, the core audio in relation to the impact event has been extracted using a command similar to the following for each video:

ffmpeg -i ../../sources/archive.org-data/1.mp4 -ss 0.766667 -to 2.233333 -acodec pcm_s16le -ar 44100 original_event_audio.wav


### Sequence

note, one video recording was unable to pick up the broadcast over the speakers. however, all other recordings capture the following same final sequence:
1. the keynote speaker says the word "violence" (note the siblence) using their microphone
2. the audience questioner follows up with saying the word "great" (in some recordings barely audible)
3. a loud bang

a spectragram have been generated along with centroid frequency & peak level db table data to help delineate between these events.
